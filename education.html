Since February 2026

Bachelor of Mechanical Engineering

Fontys University of Applied Sciences — Eindhoven, Netherlands

I started the Mechanical Engineering programme at Fontys in February 2026. The programme is applied and hands-on from the start, which suits the way I learn. Rather than spending the first year entirely in theory, you're working on real design and prototyping challenges almost immediately.

Course work so far has covered CAD modelling, mechanics, materials, thermodynamics and technical documentation. Projects have included reverse-engineering mechanical components, designing original products under constraints, and building electronics and measurement systems using Arduino and Simulink.

Engineering here is not just about solving the problem in front of you. It's about understanding why the problem exists and whether the solution you're building is actually the right one. That way of thinking is what drew me to it.

CAD / SolidWorks Mechanics Materials Science Thermodynamics Arduino / Simulink Rapid Prototyping Technical Documentation

2023 – 2025

International Baccalaureate Diploma

I.E.S. Maria Zambrano — Torre del Mar, Spain

The IB was the most demanding academic experience I've had. Two years of working across six subjects simultaneously, with a Theory of Knowledge essay, an Extended Essay and a CAS programme running alongside all of it. It teaches you to manage your own work at a level most other secondary programmes don't.

I chose a science-heavy combination with mathematics and Spanish literature, which gave me a grounding in analytical thinking, data interpretation and structured writing that I still use constantly. Physics and Maths HL in particular pushed me in the direction of engineering.

Physics HL Mathematics HL Spanish A HL History SL Chemistry SL English B HL Theory of Knowledge Extended Essay

2023 – 2025

Spanish Bachillerato

I.E.S. Maria Zambrano — Torre del Mar, Spain

The Spanish Bachillerato ran alongside the IB for the same two years, at the same school. Both programmes were completed in parallel, which meant carrying the workload of two full secondary qualifications at once.

The Bachillerato is the Spanish national pre-university qualification and was studied entirely in Spanish. It gave me formal grounding in the Spanish academic system and resulted in a qualification recognised across Spain and Europe for university admission.

Doing both at the same time was not easy, but the overlap in scientific subjects made it manageable, and the experience of switching between two very different academic frameworks in the same day is something that's genuinely useful now.
